# Stage 2 — Closest Prior Art Record

Date: 2026-09-18

## Core references

1. Bramoullé, Yann, Rachel Kranton, and Martin D'Amours (2014). “Strategic Interaction and Networks.” American Economic Review 104(3): 898–930. DOI 10.1257/aer.104.3.898.
   - Role: general LQ strategic-interaction / potential / spectral parent class.

2. Chen, Ying-Ju, Yves Zenou, and Junjie Zhou (2018). “Multiple Activities in Networks.” American Economic Journal: Microeconomics 10(3): 34–85. DOI 10.1257/mic.20160253.
   - Role: quadratic multiple-activity network-game parent class.

3. Zenou, Yves, and Junjie Zhou (2026). “Games on Multiplex Networks.” American Economic Review 116(4): 1415–1458. DOI 10.1257/aer.20240763.
   - Role: fixed aggregate effort allocated across multiple layers; closest strategy-geometry predecessor.
   - Distinction: accessible author version indicates strategic substitutes generate positive spillovers in their baseline, so it does not directly reproduce our strategic-substitutes + negative-utility-externality conjunction.

4. Bimpikis, Kostas, Asuman Ozdaglar, and Ercan Yildiz (2016). “Competitive Targeted Advertising Over Networks.” Operations Research 64(3): 705–720. DOI 10.1287/opre.2015.1430.
   - Role: competing fixed marketing budgets over targets; strategic substitutes + negative externality; unique symmetric PSNE under curvature conditions (Proposition 5).

5. Lin, Ping, and Wen Zhou (2013). “The effects of competition on the R&D portfolios of multiproduct firms.” International Journal of Industrial Organization 31(1): 83–91. DOI 10.1016/j.ijindorg.2012.11.003.
   - Role: same-product R&D strategic substitutes; cooperation internalizes negative externalities and generates stronger specialization/differentiation.

6. Dhamal, Swapnil, Walid Ben-Ameur, Tijani Chahed, and Eitan Altman (2018). “Resource Allocation Polytope Games: Uniqueness of Equilibrium, Price of Stability, and Price of Anarchy.” AAAI 2018. DOI 10.1609/aaai.v32i1.11434.
   - Role: polytope-constrained resource-allocation game; uniqueness and social optimum.

## Transportation-polytope absorption

Classical result: for a transportation polytope with positive row/column margins, the support graph of every vertex is a forest, hence an N × m_+ vertex has at most N+m_+-1 positive entries.

The Stage-1 bound k <= M-1 follows immediately once the strong-overlap fixed-margin planner is recognized as strict-convex maximization over that polytope.

Classification: **DIRECT THEOREM ABSORPTION**.
