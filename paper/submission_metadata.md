## Title

**Fixed Policy Capacity and the Duplication of Regional Industrial Policy**

## Abstract

We study competition over the composition of a predetermined, fully committed regional industrial-policy envelope. Two regional governments allocate the entire envelope across two productive sectors. Sector A has a common intrinsic advantage, while simultaneous targeting of the same sector creates a real cross-regional overlap loss. When cross-regional rivalry is below own-region curvature, the decentralized policy game has a unique equilibrium in which both regions favor sector A. A coordinator subject to the same region-specific envelopes instead assigns opposite sectoral orientations if and only if rivalry exceeds half the own-region curvature and the committed envelope satisfies B > Delta/(2 rho). Without the fixed-envelope equality constraint, the sectoral contests separate and the B-driven threshold disappears. The result isolates a narrow portfolio mechanism: the committed envelope couples otherwise separable sectoral contests, so its scale can determine whether constrained coordination favors differentiation.
