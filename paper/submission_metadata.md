## Title

**Fixed Policy Capacity and the Duplication of Regional Industrial Policy**

## Abstract

We study competition over the composition of a predetermined regional industrial-policy capacity. Two regional governments allocate a fixed capacity across two productive sectors. Sector A has a common intrinsic advantage, but simultaneous targeting of the same sector creates a real cross-regional overlap cost. When cross-regional rivalry is moderate, the decentralized policy game has a unique equilibrium in which both regions continue to favor sector A. A coordinator subject to the same region-specific capacity constraints instead assigns opposite sectoral orientations if and only if rivalry exceeds half the own-region curvature and policy capacity exceeds a closed-form threshold, B > Delta/(2 rho). Removing the fixed-capacity constraint makes the sectoral contests separable and eliminates this capacity threshold. The result shows how expanding policy capacity can raise the value of coordinating the composition, rather than the level, of regional industrial policy.
