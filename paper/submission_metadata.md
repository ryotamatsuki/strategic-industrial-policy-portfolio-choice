## Title

**Fixed Policy Capacity and the Duplication of Regional Industrial Policy**

## Abstract

We study competition over the composition of a predetermined, fully committed regional industrial-policy envelope. Two regional governments allocate the entire fixed envelope across two productive sectors. Sector A has a common intrinsic advantage, but simultaneous targeting of the same sector creates a real cross-regional overlap cost. When cross-regional rivalry is moderate, the decentralized policy game has a unique equilibrium in which both regions continue to favor sector A. A coordinator subject to the same region-specific envelope constraints instead assigns opposite sectoral orientations if and only if rivalry exceeds half the own-region curvature and the committed envelope exceeds a closed-form threshold, B > Delta/(2 rho). Removing the fixed-envelope equality constraint makes the sectoral contests separable and eliminates this capacity threshold. The result isolates a narrow composition mechanism: expanding a committed policy envelope can raise the value of coordinating where, rather than how much, regional industrial policy is directed.
